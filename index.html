RPM Home Program

What this program is for

This program builds general strength, work capacity, conditioning, movement skill, and useful athletic ability. It is not a peak program for a one-repetition maximum.

The main idea

Use the clock, but do not race it. Start work at the top of each minute. Finish the work with good form. Use the remaining time to recover.

Equipment and how to use it

The program is built around these exact constraints. Do not try to turn them into a barbell gym.

Rules that make the program work

Read these before the first session. They are part of the program, not optional notes.

Clock

Most blocks are 10-minute EMOMs. Start at the top of each minute. Do the work, then rest. If the work takes too long, reduce reps or load.

Effort

Light = about 4–5 good reps left. Medium = about 3 reps left. Heavy = about 2 reps left. Never grind a rep to finish the minute.

Speed

Move with intent. When the movement becomes slow or the technique changes, the set is too hard. Clean reps matter more than the number on the bell.

Unilateral work

For alternating-side blocks, use odd minutes for the right side and even minutes for the left side. The weaker side sets the rep target.

Sandbag

A 200 lb sandbag is a fixed, awkward load. If you cannot move it safely, do not force a lift. Use a listed kettlebell or bodyweight substitute.

Scheduling

Three training days is the normal week. Keep the four-session sequence rolling. Do not restart the program after a missed day and do not combine two sessions to catch up.

Progression

When a bell becomes too easy, first make the movement harder: more difficult leverage, cleaner pause, better range, or a harder variation. Then move to the next bell.

Recovery

Prefer at least one day between sessions when possible. After a very demanding sandbag day, take extra recovery if your next session feels poor.

3-week ramp-up

The ramp restores tolerance to training frequency, full-body work, repeated sets, and conditioning. It is not a test of how much strength you still have.

Ramp Week 13 sessions • 3 × 8-minute blocks • RPE 5–6
R1 — Squat / Row / Press

8-min goblet squat EMOM, 8-min 1-arm row alternating sides, 8-min half-kneeling 1-arm press alternating sides.

R2 — Hinge / Pull / Push

8-min single-leg RDL alternating sides, 8-min pull-up or chin-up EMOM, 8-min push-up EMOM. Finish fresh.

R3 — Split Squat / Carry / Dip

8-min split squat alternating sides, 8-min suitcase carry alternating sides, 8-min dips or assisted dips. No hard finisher.

Ramp Week 23 sessions • 3 × 10-minute blocks • RPE 5–6.5
R4 — Heavy-ish split / Carry / Press

10-min RFESS alternating sides, 10-min suitcase carry, 10-min 1-arm strict press. Add a small amount of easy core work only if recovery is excellent.

R5 — Goblet squat / Row / Dip + easy swing

10-min paused goblet squat, 10-min 1-arm row, 10-min weighted or bodyweight dips, then optional easy swings for 6 minutes.

R6 — Hinge / Pull-up / Push-up

10-min 2-hand KB swing, 10-min pull-ups or chin-ups, 10-min weighted push-ups. Keep every set crisp.

Ramp Week 33 sessions • 4 × 8–10-minute blocks • RPE 6–7
R7 — Heavy lower / Carry / Press / Core

10-min RFESS, 10-min suitcase carry, 10-min 1-arm press, 8-min hanging knee raise + easy row rotation.

R8 — Squat / Row / Dips / Conditioning

10-min goblet or front-rack squat, 10-min 1-arm row, 10-min dips, 8-min swing + easy burpee pattern.

R9 — Sandbag practice / Pull / Press / Core

8–10 min controlled sandbag carry or 1 shoulder every 2 minutes, 10-min pull-ups, 10-min 1-arm floor press, 8-min suitcase march + hanging work.

Ready for Session 1?

You should finish the ramp feeling that you could have done a little more. You should have no major soreness that carries into the next session, and your last set should still look like a good repetition.

36-session main program

Run the sessions in order. These are 36 training sessions, not 36 calendar days. At three sessions per week, the program will usually take about 12 weeks.

Session labels: H = heavy, M = medium, L = light, E = conditioning/event. “Heavy” never means maximal. This program should leave the athlete able to train again.

Exercise guide and special formats

These definitions explain the movements that are easy to misunderstand in a home setup.

Alternating-side EMOM
Minute 1: right side. Minute 2: left side. Repeat for 10 minutes. This gives five work sets per side without forcing both sides into one crowded minute.
Suitcase carry or march
Hold one kettlebell at the side. Walk 20–40 feet, or march in place if space is limited. Keep the torso quiet. Do not lean away from the weight.
Sandbag shoulder
Use a controlled ground-to-lap-to-shoulder sequence. Alternate shoulders. One clean rep is better than several rushed reps. If the bag cannot be moved safely, use the listed substitute.
Sandbag bear-hug carry
Hug the bag tightly against the torso and walk. Keep steps short and posture controlled. Start with short distances. The goal is strong, steady locomotion, not a collapse at the end.
Offset / coiling work
Use one-sided loading to make the trunk organize force while the limbs move. Do not twist violently. Keep the movement controlled and purposeful. This is a training method, not a requirement to rotate during every one-sided exercise.
Heavy, medium, and light
Heavy should feel demanding but clean, usually with about two good reps left. Medium leaves about three. Light leaves about four or five. If the bell jumps from “easy” to “too hard,” keep the same bell and use a more demanding or less demanding variation.
Bodyweight progression
Progress from bodyweight to the 25-lb vest. You can also change leverage: feet elevated, range of motion, pause, or stance. Do not chase failure.
Why this is not a barbell copy
This version keeps the original program's full-body, clock-based, undulating idea, but it uses carries, awkward objects, unilateral loading, kettlebell power work, and calisthenics because those are the best tools available in this environment.

Stop or scale the set when

You lose control of the movement, the bag becomes unsafe, the rep becomes a grind, the clock forces rushed technique, or pain changes the way you move. Reduce reps, reduce the movement difficulty, or end the block. Do not “win” the minute by using poor form.

This program is a training plan, not medical advice. Use sound judgment with the sandbag, especially when lifting it from the floor or to the shoulder. A stable training area and safe landing space for any jumping work are required.
