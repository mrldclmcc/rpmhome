Home gear strength and conditioning program

Reps per minute

A 12-week plan that builds strength and stamina, and helps you lose body fat, using only a sandbag, kettlebells, a pull-up bar, dip bars, a weighted vest, and a box.

What it is

Every session has four blocks of 10 minutes. In each block, you do a small number of good reps at the start of every minute, and you rest for the rest of that minute. The clock sets the pace.

How long it takes

First, 3 ramp-up weeks with 9 sessions. Then 36 main sessions in three blocks of 12. Train 3 or 4 times a week. Finish the sessions in order. If you miss a day, do not skip it. Do it next.

Who it is for

You have trained for years but you have been inactive for about two months. The program is made for eating a little less than you burn. It keeps your muscle and makes you fitter.

Safety. Stop a set if your form breaks down. Warm up for 5 to 10 minutes before each session. Get medical advice first if you have pain, an injury, or a health condition. Drop the sandbag only where the floor and the people around you are safe.

Equipment and how to choose loads

You have a 200 lb sandbag with no handles, kettlebells of 100, 70, 50, and 35 lb (one of each), a pull-up bar, dip bars, a 25 lb vest, and a box. You do not have pairs of kettlebells, so most exercises use one kettlebell and work one side at a time. That is a strength, not a problem: it trains your core and shows you any difference between your left and right sides.

Three intensity levels

Each exercise in the plan is marked heavy, medium, or light. The loads stay well below your maximum, so you can move fast and keep every rep clean. The last set should be harder than the first set, but it should never be a struggle.

HHeavy

The heaviest tool you can move smoothly for all ten minutes. Reps: 3-4 in Blocks 1 and 2, 2-3 in Block 3.

MMedium

A middle load, with quick reps. Reps: 4-5 in Blocks 1 and 2, 3-4 in Block 3.

LLight

A light load, with fast reps and good control. Reps: 5-6 in Blocks 1 and 2, 4-5 in Block 3.

Use the higher number of reps when you feel good and the lower number when you feel tired. Swings, jumps, and carries have their own numbers in the tables.

Ways to change difficulty between the kettlebell sizes

The jumps between 35, 50, 70, and 100 lb are large. To make an exercise harder or easier, change the position of the load (holding a kettlebell upside down is harder), add the 25 lb vest, use a pause at the hardest point of the rep, or lower the weight slowly. Add or remove these changes when you need a small step.

If a set feels too easy

Do the prescribed reps first. Then use the rest of the minute for a small extra task, such as 5 hollow rocks or 5 push-ups. This adds work and shortens your rest. Never do the extra task before the main reps.

How a session works

Every session has four blocks of 10 minutes: lower body, back, press, and core or conditioning. Use a timer. At the start of every minute, do your reps. When you finish, rest until the next minute begins. Fast reps give you more rest. Rest 1 to 2 minutes between blocks. In total, one session takes about 40 minutes of work, or 50 to 60 minutes with warm-up.

Follow this rule for every rep: never lose good form to go faster, and never lose speed to lift more weight.

Three ways to work one side at a time

1. Alternate sides by minute

Do the left side in odd minutes and the right side in even minutes. Each side gets five sets, with almost two minutes of rest between them. Use this for heavy exercises.

2. Both sides in one minute

Do all your reps on the left side, then all your reps on the right side, in the same minute. The prescribed number is per side. Use this for medium and light exercises.

3. Switch every rep

Change sides after each rep. This works well for swings, alternating lunges, and lifting the sandbag onto alternating shoulders.

If a table gives one of these formats, use it. If not, use format 1 for heavy exercises and format 2 for the others.

Finding a load when you do not know it

Start with the lightest kettlebell and move up one size every 2 minutes, while the reps stay fast and clean. Stop going up when the speed drops. Use the size just below that for the rest of the block. The ramp-up weeks use this method to find your numbers.

Ramp-up weeks: sessions R1 to R9

These three weeks get your body ready after time off, and they help you find the right loads. Train 3 times a week with at least one rest day between sessions. Every session has a warm-up, and you always finish with reps left in you. Write your results in the tracker at the bottom of this page.

The tests

  • Bear hug carry: Lift the sandbag to your chest and hug it. Walk until your arms or grip must stop. Measure the distance. If you can carry it about 50 ft, the bag is your heavy carry. If not, use it for short carries and lap lifts, and use kettlebells for long carries.
  • Box load: Load the bag from the floor onto the box. Start with a low box height. Use the highest height you can do for 3 clean reps as your heavy height.
  • Ramp tests: The load you keep for the last 2 minutes of a ramp is your working load for that exercise. Write it down.
You may repeat the ramp-up week if you feel sore or very tired. Start the main program when you have done all 9 sessions and feel fresh and ready.

Main program: 36 sessions

The program has three blocks of 12 sessions. Each block repeats the same four session types (A, B, C, D) three times. Do them in the order A, B, C, D, A, B, C, D, A, B, C, D. Each time you repeat a session type, use the progression step listed under the table.

Movement notes

Single-leg Romanian deadlift

Hold the kettlebell in the hand opposite your standing leg. Push your hips back as you lower the weight down your leg. Keep your back flat. Stand up by squeezing your glutes. If you lose balance, touch the back toe lightly to the floor as a kickstand.

Sandbag bear hug carry

Squat down, hug the bag against your chest with your arms locked tight, and stand up. Walk with short steps and keep your chest up. Put the bag down before your grip fails.

Sandbag ground to lap, lap to box

Set your feet close to the bag and get your hips low. Pull the bag up to your thighs, and then push your hips forward to bring it to lap height. For the box, drive your hips and slide the bag up onto the box. Keep your back flat and drop the bag down safely.

Sandbag ground to shoulder (test only)

Lift the bag to your lap, then pull it over one shoulder with a strong hip drive and a hug. Do this only once in the test. Use a clear floor space.

Bear hug reverse lunge

Hug the bag, and step one foot back until the back knee nearly touches the floor. Push through the front foot to stand. Use format 1 for this exercise.

One-arm strict press and Z press

Squeeze the glutes and keep your ribs down. Press the kettlebell straight up until your arm is beside your ear. For a Z press, sit on the floor with your legs straight in front of you. The lack of leg drive makes the press harder on the core.

Suitcase carry

Hold one kettlebell at your side like a suitcase. Stand tall and do not lean toward the weight. Switch hands each minute.

Two-hand and one-arm swing

Push your hips back, then snap them forward. The kettlebell rises to chest height by itself, so your arms only guide it. For a one-arm swing, change hands at the top when the kettlebell is weightless.

Goblet split squat and box step-up

Hold the kettlebell against your chest. For the split squat, place your back foot on the box and lower your back knee toward the floor. For the step-up, put your full foot on the box and stand up through the front leg only.
